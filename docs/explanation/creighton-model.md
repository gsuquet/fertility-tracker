# The Creighton Model in this application

This page tells what the Creighton Model FertilityCare System (CrMS) is, how the application uses it, and what the application does not do.

> **WARNING:** This application is not a medical device. It does not give medical advice. Use it with a certified FertilityCare Practitioner, who teaches the method and reads the chart with you.

## What the Creighton Model is

The Creighton Model is a standard method to observe and record the biological signs of fertility. Dr. Thomas W. Hilgers developed it at the Saint Paul VI Institute for the Study of Human Reproduction. The user records bleeding and cervical mucus each day with standard codes.

The method does not calculate fertile days from a calendar. It uses only what the user sees each day. Thus, it works for regular and irregular cycles.

## Why mucus is the main sign

Before ovulation, the hormone estrogen increases. The cervix then makes mucus that becomes clear, stretchy and lubricative. This mucus helps sperm to live and to move. After ovulation, the hormone progesterone stops this mucus. The days become dry again.

Thus, fertile mucus shows the fertile days. The last day of fertile mucus is the Peak Day. Ovulation usually occurs near the Peak Day.

## Why the chart uses stamps

On a paper chart, the user puts a colored stamp on each day. The color shows the type of day at a glance: red for bleeding, green for dry days, white with a baby for fertile days. The application gives the same stamps automatically from the codes. Thus, the screen looks like the paper chart that a practitioner knows.

## Why there is a count of three

Fertility does not stop on the last day of fertile mucus. The method counts three more days after it. These days stay fertile, and the chart shows them with a light green stamp and the numbers 1, 2 and 3.

The count applies after each patch of fertile mucus, not only after the Peak Day. A short patch of mucus before the Peak Day can also show a fertile time. When mucus comes back during the count, the count starts again.

## Why you can set the Peak Day

The automatic rule takes the last day of fertile mucus in the cycle. Some cycles are not that simple. For example, a cycle can have two patches of fertile mucus, or a continuous discharge. In these cycles, a practitioner decides the Peak Day. Thus, the application lets you set the Peak Day yourself.

## What the application does not do

- It does not give the yellow stamps of the continuous mucus rules. The welcome guide shows them for information only.
- It does not tell you if a day is good or bad for a pregnancy. It shows the signs. You and your practitioner decide.
- It does not replace the training of the method.
